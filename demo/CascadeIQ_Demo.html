Methodology

Physics + AI hybrid approach for multi-hazard cascade prediction

1. The Gap We're Closing

India's disaster agencies (IMD, GSI, CWC) already forecast individual hazards well. CascadeIQ is a connector layer, not a competing predictor. It ingests those forecasts and computes the probability that one hazard triggers another downstream — closing the integration gap.

Research context: 55% of AI early-warning literature focuses on forecasting; only 1% addresses response preparedness; only 4 of 324 reviewed multi-hazard papers model hazards as causal chains rather than independent events.

2. Physics + AI Split (the key architectural decision)
Physics Module Defines causal structure and physical plausibility:
• Korup (2002) dam-blockage threshold
• Caine (1980) rainfall trigger (Himalayan-calibrated)
• Kinematic wave flood travel time (Huss 2007)

Pure functions — no learned parameters.
Bayesian Network (AI) Calibrates probabilities on physics-constrained structure:
• pgmpy BayesianNetwork (DAG)
• Nodes: Rain → Soil → Slope → Landslide → Blockage → Flood
• Physics-informed CPD priors
• Variable Elimination inference

4. Backtest — Pune 2019 GLOF

The historical replay reconstructs pre-event data (Pune Extreme Rainfall, 4 Oct 2023) from Sattar et al. (2024). CascadeIQ raises ORANGE at T-24h and RED at T-12h — 12 hours before the confirmed event. Flood travel time to Singtam was ~3–6h, giving ~6–9h net warning window.

5. Explicit Non-Goals
  • Not replacing IMD/GSI/CWC — it is a connector layer on top of them.
  • Not addressing Wayanad-type warning-to-action failures — different system.
  • Not national coverage — one hazard pair, one pilot region.
  • Not doing individual-level health prediction.
6. References
